## 1. My BRRRR Buy Box + Agent & Seller Scripts

### Most people start by browsing houses. That's backwards.

Your buy box decides whether the numbers can work at all. Pick the box first, then the house. If a deal doesn't fit, you don't fall in love with it. You pass and move on.

### My BRRRR buy box

| Criteria | What I look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Property type** | 2–4 unit residential (small multifamily), plus value-add SFH in strong rental pockets | Residential financing on up to 4 units, multiple rents per roof |
| **Condition** | Dated, ugly, tired, but structurally sound | The value is in the rehab. Ugly scares off retail buyers |
| **Purchase price** | All-in (price + rehab) **≤ 75% of ARV** | Lets the refinance pull most or all of your cash back out |
| **Rent** | Monthly rent **≥ 1% of all-in cost** as a first filter | Quick sanity check before full underwriting |
| **Cash flow after refi** | **$[X] / door / month** minimum after PITI, management, CapEx and vacancy reserves | If it doesn't cash flow after the refi, it's not a BRRRR, it's a gamble |
| **Location** | Within [X] minutes of my contractor + property manager, B/C+ neighborhoods, no flood zones | You can't BRRRR without a team that can get to the property |
| **Seller situation** | Tired landlords, estates, mom-and-pop owners, deferred maintenance | Motivated sellers = better price, better terms |

### Instant passes

- Rehab needs I can't scope from photos plus one walkthrough (foundation, full gut with structural)
- Rent comps I can't verify with at least 3 real leases or listings
- Deals that only work at 100% occupancy and zero CapEx
- "It'll appreciate". Appreciation is a bonus, never the plan.

### Agent script (first call)

```
Hey [Agent], my name is [Name]. I'm an active investor buying 2–4 unit properties in [market].

I'm not a tire kicker. I close, and I'll be a repeat client.

Here's my box: [units], [price range], needs work, all-in under 75% of ARV. I'm happy to look at stuff that's ugly or been sitting.

Can you set me up on an auto-search for that, and flag anything off-market or with a tired landlord? If you bring me a deal that fits, I can move fast and I'll use you on the resale or the next one.
```

### Seller script (mom-and-pop landlord)

```
Hi [Owner], my name is [Name]. I'm a local investor and I came across your property at [address].

I'm not a big company. I buy a few buildings a year and I take care of them.

Have you ever thought about what it would look like to be done being a landlord?

[Listen. Let them talk about tenants, repairs, taxes, family.]

If the number made sense, would you be open to some flexibility on how you get paid? Some owners like getting a monthly check instead of one big tax bill.
```

> That last line opens the **seller financing** conversation. More on that in *Why BRRRR*.

### Follow-up text (no answer)

## 2. Why BRRRR: The Playbook Behind My First 30+ Cycles

### The problem with "save up and buy"

If you save $50K, put 25% down on a rental, and wait for it to cash flow, you get **one** property. Then you start saving again. At a teacher's salary, that's a property every few years. That's not freedom. That's a hobby.

### BRRRR flips it

**Buy → Rehab → Rent → Refinance → Repeat.** You force the value up, then refinance against the *new* value to pull your capital back out, so the same dollars can do the next deal.

### A worked example (illustrative)

| Step | Number |
|---|---|
| Buy (3-family, tired) | $289,000 |
| Rehab | $62,000 |
| **All-in** | **$351,000** |
| After-repair value (ARV) | $470,000 |
| Refinance at 75% LTV | $352,500 |
| **Cash left in the deal** | **≈ $0** |
| Rent (3 units) | $4,350 / mo |
| **Equity created** | **≈ $117,500** |

You end up owning a cash-flowing 3-family with roughly none of your own money left in it, and that money is free to go into deal #2.

### The 5 steps, and where people mess them up

1. **Buy.** Make money on the buy. Your profit is locked in at purchase, not at sale. Mistake: buying retail and "hoping" the rehab adds value.
2. **Rehab.** Rehab for the renter and the appraiser, not for you. Kitchens, baths, flooring, paint, curb appeal. Mistake: over-improving, or no contingency (budget 10–15%).
3. **Rent.** Rent it at market to a screened tenant *before* you refi. Lenders want to see the lease. Mistake: rushing a bad tenant in to hit a refi date.
4. **Refinance.** Know your lender's seasoning period, LTV and DSCR requirement *before you buy*. Mistake: finding out after the rehab that the bank wants 12 months of seasoning.
5. **Repeat.** Systemize it: same buy box, same contractor, same PM, same lender. That's how one deal becomes thirty.

### Where the money comes from when you don't have it

- **Private money:** people in your network who want a better return than their savings account. Raise it with a deal in hand, not an idea.
- **Hard money:** short-term purchase + rehab financing, then refi out.
- **Seller financing:** the seller becomes the bank. Great for tired landlords who want income instead of a big tax bill.
- **Partners:** someone brings the capital, you bring the deal and the work.

## 3. My AI Deal-Finding & Underwriting Prompts

Use these in ChatGPT, Claude or whatever AI assistant you use. **AI is your analyst, not your decision-maker.** Verify every number it gives you with real comps, a real contractor and a real lender.

### Prompt 1: Market screen

```
Act as a real estate investment analyst. I'm looking to BRRRR 2–4 unit properties.
Compare these markets: [City A], [City B], [City C].
For each, give me: median price for a 2–4 unit, typical rent per unit, rent-to-price ratio, population and job trend, landlord-friendliness, and property tax rate.
Put it in a table, flag anything that would worry an investor, and tell me which data I should verify myself and where.
```

### Prompt 2: Listing quick-kill

```
Here's a listing: [paste description, price, units, rents, photos description].
Using this buy box — all-in ≤ 75% of ARV, rent ≥ 1% of all-in, min $[X]/door cash flow after refi — tell me in 5 bullets whether this is worth a walkthrough, what the max offer would need to be to fit, and what questions I should ask the agent.
```

### Prompt 3: Rehab budget from photos

```
I'm uploading listing photos of a [year] [type] in [city].
Estimate a rehab scope and budget in three tiers (light / medium / heavy) by room and system: kitchen, baths, flooring, paint, roof, HVAC, electrical, plumbing, exterior.
Add a 15% contingency. List what you can't see from photos that my contractor must check.
```

### Prompt 4: BRRRR math

```
Run BRRRR numbers:
Purchase $[ ], rehab $[ ], closing costs $[ ], holding costs $[ ]/mo for [ ] months, ARV $[ ], refi LTV [ ]%, rate [ ]%, 30-yr, rents $[ ]/unit x [ ] units, taxes $[ ]/yr, insurance $[ ]/yr, management [ ]%, vacancy [ ]%, CapEx [ ]%.
Show: total cash in, refi loan amount, cash left in deal, monthly cash flow, cash-on-cash return, and DSCR. Show your math.
```

### Prompt 5: Seller letter

```
Write a short, warm, non-salesy letter to the owner of [address], a [type] they've owned for [X] years.
I'm a local investor, I buy a few buildings a year, I can buy as-is and be flexible on terms including seller financing.
Keep it under 150 words, plain language, no hype.
```

### Prompt 6: Agent outreach

```
Write a text and a short email to a local investor-friendly agent in [market] introducing me as an active buyer of 2–4 unit value-add properties.
Include my buy box: [criteria]. Make it clear I close and will be a repeat client. Under 100 words each.
```
